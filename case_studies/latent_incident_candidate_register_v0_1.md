## Method and promotion threshold

Discovery used lexical signals only to locate candidate-bearing passages. Each candidate was then read in context, deduplicated by trigger, mechanism, and repair/restart boundary, and compared with the register above.

A candidate is `NEW CONFIRMED INCIDENT` only when all of the following are bound:

1. a bounded trigger;
2. an occurred, not merely predicted, failure;
3. a distinct failure mechanism;
4. a repair, restart result, or unresolved stop state;
5. an exact evidence location; and
6. enough source identity to separate the worldline from existing cases.

The exact classification vocabulary is:

- `NEW CONFIRMED INCIDENT`
- `DUPLICATE OF EXISTING CASE`
- `PART OF EXISTING WORLDLINE`
- `ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE FOR EXISTING CASE`
- `NEAR-MISS / SUCCESSFUL CONTAINMENT`
- `CONTROLLED VALIDATION CASE`
- `GENERAL RISK / HYPOTHESIS`
- `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE`
